About the Journal

Facades Tell Stories is an independent publication exploring the intersection of interior design, architecture, and the lived experience of buildings.

Founded in 2023 by architect and writer Elena Marsh, Facades Tell Stories began as a personal notebook—a place to collect observations about the way buildings speak to us before we even step inside. That notebook grew into a journal read by homeowners, design professionals, and anyone who believes that the built environment shapes how we feel, move, and live.

We publish in-depth essays, practical guides, and critical perspectives. Our focus is always on materiality, light, and the stories that spaces tell—whether it's a timber facade that silvers with age, a lime-plastered wall that breathes with the seasons, or a stairwell that turns a simple climb into a moment of ceremony.

“The best facades don't shout; they invite you to look closer.”
A facade is the first chapter of any building's story.

What We Believe

We believe that good design is not about trends—it's about attention. Attention to how a room feels at different times of day. Attention to the weight of a door handle, the texture of a stone countertop, the path of a shadow across a wall. We believe that materials should age gracefully, that light is a building material, and that negative space is never wasted.

Most of all, we believe that buildings are not silent. They tell stories about the people who live in them, the climate they respond to, and the culture that shaped them. Our job is to listen closely and share what we hear.

Our Editorial Approach

Every article begins with a question. Why does this room feel calm? How does this facade handle the afternoon sun? What happens when you choose lime plaster over gypsum board? We answer these questions through reporting, interviews, and first-hand visits—never through press releases or sponsored content.

We are reader-supported and proudly independent. We do not accept advertising that compromises our editorial integrity. When we recommend a material or a designer, it's because we've seen it work in the real world.
